## 1. How malleable are spatial skills? Meta-analyses (Uttal 2013, Hawes 2022, later work) and replication concerns

### Takeaway
Spatial skills are robustly trainable: the main meta-analysis (Uttal et al., 2013; 217 studies) finds a medium effect (g ≈ 0.47) that persists over weeks to months and transfers to untrained spatial tasks. Transfer to *mathematics* is real but smaller (g ≈ 0.28; Hawes et al., 2022) and inconsistent in individual studies, especially with brief mental-rotation-only training in young children.

### Cited Findings
**Uttal et al. (2013)** — Uttal, D. H., Meadow, N. G., Tipton, E., Hand, L. L., Alden, A. R., Warren, C., & Newcombe, N. S. The malleability of spatial skills: A meta-analysis of training studies. *Psychological Bulletin, 139*(2), 352–402. doi:10.1037/a0028446
- Meta-analysed 217 studies on the magnitude, moderators, durability and generalizability of spatial training; conclusion: spatial skills are malleable and training can transfer — [PubMed 22663761](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22663761/)
- Average effect of training relative to control ≈ g = 0.47 (reported in the abstract and in secondary summaries; full text not fetched) — [PubMed 22663761](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22663761/); secondary figure also given in [Northwestern summary PDF](https://groups.psych.northwestern.edu/uttal/research/documents/TrainingSpatialSkills.pdf)
- Age breakdown reported secondhand: children g ≈ 0.61, adolescents ≈ 0.44, adults ≈ 0.44 — i.e., adults still benefit substantially — [search summary citing Uttal et al.](https://groups.psych.northwestern.edu/uttal/research/documents/TrainingSpatialSkills.pdf) (secondary; verify)
- Durability: effects lasted in studies with delayed post-tests (up to ~4 months in some); immediate vs delayed effects not significantly different — [Temple/EurekAlert release](https://www.eurekalert.org/news-releases/564016); [Northwestern news](https://news.northwestern.edu/stories/2012/08/uttal-spatial-thinking)
- Transfer: similar effect sizes for overall improvement, near-transfer and far-transfer tasks; authors' own hedge: results "minimally show that durable, medium transfer is possible" — [EurekAlert](https://www.eurekalert.org/news-releases/564016); [Uttal summary PDF](https://groups.psych.northwestern.edu/uttal/research/documents/TrainingSpatialSkills.pdf)
- **[recall — verify]** Control groups also improved substantially (g ≈ 0.45) from repeated testing alone, so the pre-post gains of trained groups overstate the training-specific effect; training-type subgroups were similar (video games ≈ 0.54, semester courses ≈ 0.41, spatial task practice ≈ 0.48); men and women improved similarly; authors argued training could roughly double the number of people with spatial ability above the typical engineer's threshold — [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22663761/)

**Hawes, Gilligan-Lee & Mix (2022)** — Effects of spatial training on mathematics performance: A meta-analysis. *Developmental Psychology, 58*(1), 112–137. doi:10.1037/dev0001281
- 29 controlled pre-post studies, N = 3,765, k = 89 effect sizes — [Surrey Open Research record](https://openresearch.surrey.ac.uk/esploro/outputs/journalArticle/Effects-of-Spatial-Training-on-Mathematics/99783428802346); [Bohrium summary](https://www.bohrium.com/paper-details/effects-of-spatial-training-on-mathematics-performance-a-meta-analysis/814553438133157903-11364)
- Spatial training improved spatial thinking g = .49 (SE = .09) and mathematics g ≈ .28 (SE = .07) — [Bohrium summary](https://www.bohrium.com/paper-details/effects-of-spatial-training-on-mathematics-performance-a-meta-analysis/814553438133157903-11364)
- Moderators: age (larger effects with age, 3–20 y), **concrete manipulatives > non-concrete (e.g., computerized) training**, near > far transfer; no significant publication bias or selective-reporting signal — [same](https://www.bohrium.com/paper-details/effects-of-spatial-training-on-mathematics-performance-a-meta-analysis/814553438133157903-11364)
- Caveats: only 5 of 29 studies had participants older than 12, so the age moderator for teens/adults rests on thin data; mechanisms of transfer poorly understood — [same](https://www.bohrium.com/paper-details/effects-of-spatial-training-on-mathematics-performance-a-meta-analysis/814553438133157903-11364)

**Mental-rotation → math transfer: replication problems**
- Cheng & Mix (2014) reported that a single session of mental rotation training improved 6–8-year-olds' math (missing-term problems); Hawes, Moss, Caswell & Poliszczuk (2015, *Trends in Neuroscience and Education* 4:60–68), a 6-week RCT (3×/week, 15–20 min, vs literacy control), improved trained mental rotation and an untrained mental transformation task but found **no transfer to mathematics**; Cornu et al. (2017) and Xu & LeFevre (2016) also failed to replicate — [PMC10299554 summary via search](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10299554); [Springer Psych Research 2019](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00426-019-01202-5)
- Cheung, Sung & Lourenco (2019) at-home visuospatial intervention: mental rotation training did not clearly transfer to math — [PubMed 31144028](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31144028/)
- 2025 study (N = 80 children, active control): short mental-rotation training improved overall arithmetic and reduced counting strategies; state-anxiety reduction proposed as mechanism — [NSF PAR](https://par.nsf.gov/servlets/purl/10627420)

**Judd & Klingberg (2021)** — Training spatial cognition enhances mathematical learning in a randomized study of 17,000 children. *Nature Human Behaviour, 5*, 1548–1554. doi:10.1038/s41562-021-01118-4
- 17,648 children aged 6–8, 7 weeks of math training plus randomly assigned spatial training — [Karolinska news](https://news.ki.se/these-cognitive-exercises-help-children-boost-their-math-skills-study-shows); [IDEAS/RePEc](https://ideas.repec.org/a/nat/nathum/v5y2021i11d10.1038_s41562-021-01118-4.html)
- Visuospatial working memory and reasoning training gave the largest math gains; both outperformed **mental rotation** training; benefit varied ~threefold between individuals; no passive control group; author Klingberg is unpaid CSO of the non-profit owning the Vektor app used — [ScienceDaily](https://www.sciencedaily.com/releases/2021/05/210520133755.htm); [PsyPost](https://www.psypost.org/a-massive-psychology-study-has-found-that-spatial-cognition-training-can-enhance-childrens-math-learning/)

### Inferences
- Robust: spatial skills improve with practice/training at a medium effect size, with gains lasting at least weeks to months. Contested: how much of that is test-specific practice (control groups improve a lot), and whether short, narrow training (e.g., mental rotation alone) transfers to math.
- For a geometry/drawing tutor, the best-supported design is sustained, varied spatial practice embedded in the target content (spatial reasoning *about geometry*), not isolated rotation drills expected to "spill over".
- Near transfer (to other spatial tasks, e.g., from rotation to cross-sections) is more dependable than far transfer (to math grades).

### Gaps
- Could not fetch Uttal 2013 full text to confirm subgroup numbers (control-group gain, training-type breakdown, sex moderator, trim-and-fill result).
- Found no new large general spatial-training meta-analysis from 2024–2026 that supersedes Uttal 2013; a Springer *Educational Psychology Review* 2026 article (doi:10.1007/s10648-026-10149-0) appeared in results but its content was not retrieved — [link](https://link.springer.com/10.1007/s10648-026-10149-0).
- No formal multi-lab replication of Uttal-style training effects found.

## 2. What is "spatial ability"? Mental rotation, Newcombe & Shipley typology, penetrative thinking, perspective taking

### Takeaway
Spatial ability is not one skill. The dominant framework splits it into intrinsic (within-object) vs extrinsic (between objects/frames) and static vs dynamic; intrinsic–extrinsic is supported by developmental data, static–dynamic much less. Mental rotation is the best-studied skill, but cross-sectioning (penetrative thinking) — the core skill for technical drawing — is psychologically distinct.

### Cited Findings
- Shepard & Metzler (1971), "Mental rotation of three-dimensional objects," *Science* 171(3972):701–703: time to judge two 3D block figures as identical increases linearly with angular disparity, implying an analog "mental rotation" process — [cogn-iq overview](https://www.cogn-iq.org/learn/theory/mental-rotation/); original doi:10.1126/science.171.3972.701
- Newcombe & Shipley (2015), "Thinking about spatial thinking: New typology, new assessments," in *Studying Visual and Spatial Reasoning for Design Creativity* (Springer) proposed the 2×2 classification intrinsic vs extrinsic × static vs dynamic (with Uttal et al.) — [Springer chapter](https://link.springer.com/10.1007/978-94-017-9297-4_10); [Mix et al. trajectories paper, PMC8519147](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC8519147/)
- In 6–11-year-olds (N = 184), developmental trajectories of intrinsic vs extrinsic skills differed significantly, but there was "little evidence" that static vs dynamic dimensions psychometrically dissociate (static may be prerequisite for dynamic) — [PMC8519147](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC8519147/)
- Newcombe, Uttal & Sauter (2013) frame intrinsic (intra-object representations and their transformation, e.g., mental rotation) and extrinsic (inter-object representations used for navigation) as two developmental strands — [PMC8519147](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC8519147/)
- Penetrative thinking = visualizing spatial relations inside an object (e.g., drawing a slice through a block); geoscience researchers note it is not the same as mental rotation and may need different processes, so rotation training may not transfer directly — [SERC Spatial Workbook, prior research](https://serc.carleton.edu/spatialworkbook/prior_research.html); [SERC about](https://serc.carleton.edu/spatialworkbook/about.html)

### Inferences
- Drawing/descriptive geometry heavily load intrinsic-dynamic skills (rotating, cutting, folding, projecting views); perspective taking (extrinsic) matters for viewpoints/orthographic views. A tutor should train and assess these separately rather than assume one "spatial IQ".

### Gaps
- No meta-analytic data retrieved specifically on training perspective taking (e.g., Hegarty & Waller perspective-taking test) in this session.

## 3. Which interventions work? (Sorby, video games, blocks/LEGO, origami, sketching, gesture, spatial language, VR/AR, physical vs screen)

### Takeaway
Best evidence for older students: Sorby-style structured spatial-visualization courses (sketching isometric/orthographic views, rotations, cross-sections) for low scorers, with downstream gains in STEM grades and retention. Video-game effects exist but are inflated by publication bias. Physical/concrete manipulation and producing gestures beat merely watching; sketch-then-compare feedback improves cross-sectioning. VR/AR show medium pooled effects but with heterogeneous, lower-quality evidence.

### Cited Findings
**Sorby's spatial visualization training (engineering)**
- Sorby, Veurink & Streiner (2018), "Does spatial skills instruction improve STEM outcomes? The answer is 'yes'," *Learning and Individual Differences* 67: >3,000 first-year engineering students over 5 years, assignment by pass/fail cutoff on spatial test (PSVT:R); training improved STEM retention, **particularly for women**, and grades in a variety of STEM courses — [Michigan Tech Digital Commons](https://digitalcommons.mtu.edu/michigantech-p/6855/); [Rowan record](https://www.researchwithrowan.com/en/publications/does-spatial-skills-instruction-improve-stem-outcomes-the-answer-/)
- Earlier retrospective longitudinal work: for students with initially weak 3D spatial skills, the intervention improved course grades and engineering retention — [ASEE PEER 18592](https://peer.asee.org/18592.pdf); [Sorby & Veurink EDGD paper on students who left](https://edgd.asee.org/wp-content/uploads/sites/22/2019/09/Impact-of-Visualization-Training-on-Student-Leaving_Sorby-Veurink_67th-EDGD-Proceedings.pdf)
- Caveat: design is a regression-discontinuity-like cutoff, not randomization; smaller replications (e.g., Penn State Behrend) had few women (24 vs 303 men) — [Blasko et al. longitudinal analysis](https://researchgate.net/profile/Dawn-Blasko/publication/266170923_Longitudinal_Analysis_of_Spatial_Skills_Training_in_Engineering_Graphics/links/54cf93ca0cf29ca810fee7e1/Longitudinal-Analysis-of-Spatial-Skills-Training-in-Engineering-Graphics.pdf)

**Video games**
- Feng, Spence & Pratt (2007), *Psychological Science* 18(10):850–855: 10 h of an action video game virtually eliminated a gender difference in spatial attention and narrowed the mental-rotation gap, with women benefiting more; non-action-game control showed no gain (small sample) — [APS](https://www.psychologicalscience.org/journals/psychological-science/j.1467-9280.2007.01990.x/); [ScienceDaily](https://sciencedaily.com/releases/2007/10/071024145626.htm)
- Terlecki, Newcombe & Little (2008), *Applied Cognitive Psychology* 22:996–1013: undergraduates trained with Tetris or repeated MRT testing; gains maintained several months; Tetris showed faster early growth and greater transfer to other spatial tasks that persisted months, but final MRT scores did not differ from repeated testing; men and women did not converge (men grew faster early, women improved more later) — [Zendy record](https://zendy.io/title/10.1002/acp.1420); [CAS catalogue](https://katalog.lib.cas.cz/KNAV/EdsRecord/edsair,edsair.doi...........e6ea9c38500405fa0f7fc3752bfe3df4)
- Bediou et al. (2018, *Psychological Bulletin*): action video games enhance top-down attention and spatial cognition; intervention studies g = 0.34; authors estimated published effects ~30% larger than full literature — [record](https://git.lsit.ucsb.edu/publicdata/nih-gov/raw/commit/a3492137cada35c1427e5783c176e4344e6cbb56/pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29172564/index.html)
- Hilgard, Sala, Boot & Simons (2019, *Collabra: Psychology*): re-analysis with stronger bias correction found minimal evidence of transfer to cognitive measures; larger effects from one lab; overlapping samples across papers ("salami slicing") — [DOAJ](https://doaj.org/article/883998e0285146c2bdaaa6315879c0fc); [Illinois Experts](https://experts.illinois.edu/en/publications/overestimation-of-action-game-training-effects-publication-bias-a/)

**Blocks, LEGO, origami (younger learners)**
- LEGO RCT (*Developmental Science*, 2024): 198 children aged 7–9 randomized to physical LEGO (n=59), digital LEGO (n=64), or crafts control (n=75), 6 weeks twice weekly — outcomes not retrieved — [AMiner](https://www.aminer.org/pub/64af72533fda6d7f0643d1f9/assessing-the-impact-of-lego-construction-training-on-spatial-and-mathematical-skills); [Surrey BLOCS project](https://www.surrey.ac.uk/block-construction-skills-mathematics-blocs/about-our-research)
- Teacher-led LEGO SPACE programme (UK, n = 409, 6 weeks): reported gains in mental rotation and maths (press summary; Farran et al., 2025, *Mind, Brain, and Education*) — [AlphaGalileo](https://www.alphagalileo.org/fr-fr/Item-Display-fr-FR/ItemId/259754)
- Origami (Project VisMO): RCT with 179 elementary students showed gains in spatial vocabulary and reduced math anxiety; follow-ups suggest possible geometry gains — [Frontiers in Education 2025](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/education/articles/10.3389/feduc.2025.1613317/pdf)
- Unstructured block play evidence is mainly correlational — [CORE](https://core.ac.uk/outputs/673488590)

**Physical manipulatives vs screen**
- Hawes et al. (2022) meta-analysis: training with concrete manipulatives produced larger math transfer than non-concrete (e.g., computerized) training — [Bohrium summary](https://www.bohrium.com/paper-details/effects-of-spatial-training-on-mathematics-performance-a-meta-analysis/814553438133157903-11364)

**Sketching / drawing**
- Gagnier & Shipley: students predicted (sketched) cross-sections of geologic block diagrams, then compared with the actual cross-section aligned to the uncut block; significant pre-post gains on the Geologic Block Cross-sectioning Test in the experimental but not control condition — [SERC prior research](https://serc.carleton.edu/spatialworkbook/prior_research.html); [GSA 2013 poster](https://gsa.confex.com/gsa/2013AM/webprogram/Handout/Paper228604/2013%20GSA%20poster.pdf)
- Ormand et al.: ~10% improvement across mental rotation, penetrative thinking and disembedding after a geoscience course; curriculum uses predictive sketching, visual comparison, gesture and analogy — [SERC](https://serc.carleton.edu/spatialworkbook/prior_research.html)

**Gesture**
- Chu & Kita (2011), *J. Exp. Psychol.: General* 140:102–116: adults encouraged to gesture solved more mental rotation problems than gesture-allowed or gesture-prohibited groups; gesturing declined with experience, and gesture-trained participants still outperformed others when later prohibited from gesturing (internalization) — [Birmingham repository PDF](https://research.birmingham.ac.uk/files/17533903/Kita_Journal_of_Experimental_Psychology_2011.pdf); [memory-key summary](https://www.memory-key.com/archive/research/news/gestures-provide-helping-hand-problem-solving?page=4)
- Goldin-Meadow et al. (2012): 158 six-year-olds; producing a "Move" gesture improved mental transformation more than watching it; a Point gesture did not help — doing > seeing, and only if the gesture carries task-relevant information — [ScholarHub](https://scholarhub.vn/publication/Doing-gesture-promotes-learning-a-mental-transformation-task-better-than-seeing-gesture/080c62fc-9398-4084-a1d1-a83fca8aacd1); [PMC3984351](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3984351)
- Preschool study: training children to gesture about rotation improves mental rotation (compared with joystick manual rotation) — [CogSci proceedings](https://repositories.cdlib.org/content/qt75q80579/qt75q80579.pdf?t=op2ln5)

**VR / AR**
- Di & Zheng (2022, *ETR&D*): meta-analysis of virtual technologies, pooled medium effect ≈ 0.62 on spatial ability; larger for preschool learners and science/engineering; AR most effective among virtual technologies — [BVB record](https://bvbvf1.bib-bvb.de/vufind/PrimoRecord/cdi_proquest_journals_2640562020)
- Bölek et al. (2021, *Scientific Reports*): AR in anatomy, only 5 studies, no significant difference vs controls — [DOAJ](https://doaj.org/article/b1e72b1d932d4184b8a74d91a88376ed)

### Inferences
- For a university-level drawing course: a Sorby-type module (≈10–15 h of sketching rotations, orthographic/isometric conversion, cross-sections, folding) aimed at students scoring low on PSVT:R is the best-evidenced option.
- "Predict → sketch → compare with the true answer" is a strong tutor loop (sketching + immediate visual feedback), as is asking learners to *make* gestures/physical models rather than watch animations.
- Commercial games and VR should be treated as optional supplements; their effects are inflated or heterogeneous.

### Gaps
- Spatial language interventions (e.g., Pruden, Levine & Huttenlocher 2011, doi:10.1111/j.1467-7687.2011.01088.x) **[recall — verify]**: parental spatial-word use predicts children's spatial skill; causal training evidence not retrieved here beyond Project VisMO.
- No retrieved meta-analysis directly comparing haptic interventions or physical vs digital manipulation for adults; LEGO RCT physical-vs-digital outcomes not obtained.
- No independent replication of Feng et al. 2007 located in this session.

## 4. Critical periods vs adult plasticity — does training at university age still work?

### Takeaway
No evidence of a closed critical period for spatial skills: adults and university students show training gains comparable in size to adolescents (Uttal), and the strongest real-world outcomes (grades, retention) come from university engineering courses (Sorby). Children may gain somewhat more, but math-transfer data for teens/adults are sparse.

### Cited Findings
- Uttal 2013 age breakdown (secondary): children ≈ 0.61, adolescents ≈ 0.44, adults ≈ 0.44 — [Uttal summary PDF](https://groups.psych.northwestern.edu/uttal/research/documents/TrainingSpatialSkills.pdf)
- Hawes 2022: math transfer grew with age across 3–20 years, but only 5 studies >12 y — [Bohrium](https://www.bohrium.com/paper-details/effects-of-spatial-training-on-mathematics-performance-a-meta-analysis/814553438133157903-11364)
- University-level training improves grades and retention (Sorby et al. 2018) — [MTU](https://digitalcommons.mtu.edu/michigantech-p/6855/)
- Undergraduates show durable months-long gains from Tetris/repeated testing (Terlecki et al. 2008) — [Zendy](https://zendy.io/title/10.1002/acp.1420)

### Inferences
- Training a university-age audience is evidence-based; prioritise low-baseline learners, who gain most and for whom the outcome payoff is documented.

### Gaps
- No neuroscientific critical-period evidence for spatial transformation skills was retrieved; claims about "windows" in childhood should be treated as unsupported.

## 5. Gender differences in mental rotation: size, trends, training, stereotype threat

### Takeaway
Mental rotation shows the largest and most reliable sex difference among cognitive spatial measures (d ≈ 0.5–0.7 in adults, varies with test format, timing), absent in preschool, small at 6–8, and growing through adolescence. It appears in all 53 nations surveyed and is larger in more gender-equal/developed countries. Training improves both sexes; it narrows but usually does not erase the gap. Stereotype threat effects on spatial tasks are small/inconsistent; stereotype "lift" for women shows more.

### Cited Findings
- Lauer, Yhang & Lourenco (2019), *Psychological Bulletin* (doi:10.1037/bul0000191): 303 effect sizes, 30,613 children/adolescents; small male advantage in childhood strengthens to moderate by adolescence; task and stimulus characteristics moderate the gap — [PubMed 30973235](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30973235/); no gap among preschoolers, small gap ages 6–8, roughly tripling by late adolescence — [Emory News](https://news.emory.edu/stories/2019/04/esc_gender_gap_spatial_reasoning/campus.html); [AAU](https://www.aau.edu/node/26051)
- Adult MR difference: Voyer et al. meta-analysis d = 0.56; MR range d = 0.56–0.73, most reliable sex difference among spatial measures — [cited in Frontiers/PMC literature via search](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9488019)
- Lippa, Collaer & Peters (2010, *Archives of Sexual Behavior*; BBC Internet survey, >90,000 women, >111,000 men, 53 nations): men outperformed women on mental rotation in every nation; greater gender equality/economic development associated with *larger* differences (published online 2009; doi:10.1007/s10508-008-9430-6 **[recall — verify DOI]**) — finding surfaced via search snippet; the hosting record ([Open University ORO](https://oro.open.ac.uk/49902)) was not opened, so confirm attribution against the original
- Trends over time conflict: a 1993 *Behavior Genetics* paper ("Is the gender difference in mental rotation disappearing?") found MRT difference stable over time; Richardson (1994) argued it shrank over 20 years — [Scilit](https://scilit.net/publications/f0489fdc4b69fdf3fb2d0a30c80b8dfd); [JioInstitute record](https://searchworks.jioinstitute.edu.in/articles/cmedm__8240213)
- Training & the gap: Feng et al. (2007) — women benefited more from action-game training, narrowing the MR gap — [APS](https://www.psychologicalscience.org/journals/psychological-science/j.1467-9280.2007.01990.x/); Terlecki et al. (2008) — men and women did not converge — [Zendy](https://zendy.io/title/10.1002/acp.1420); Sorby et al. (2018) — retention benefits particularly for women — [MTU](https://digitalcommons.mtu.edu/michigantech-p/6855/)
- Stereotype effects: Doyle & Voyer (2016, *Learning and Individual Differences*, doi:10.1016/j.lindif.2015.12.018), 224 effect sizes from 86 studies; significant stereotype *lift* for females on spatial tasks (≈ −0.39) while stereotype *threat* effect (≈ 0.12) not significant; others describe results as variable/null — [Rahe et al. 2023, PMC10226650](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC10226650/); [Guizzo et al.](https://www.research.unipd.it/retrieve/e14fb26c-02d9-3de1-e053-1705fe0ac030/19_Guizzo%20et%20al_The%20role%20of%20implicit%20gender%20spatial%20stereotyping%20in%20mental%20rotation%20performance.pdf); Rahe et al. argue self-concept explains gender differences after stereotype activation — [PMC10226650](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC10226650/)
- Test format (item type, time limits) changes gap size — [EDGJ](https://www.edgj.org/index.php/EDGJ/article/view/115/111); [time limits paper](https://typeset.io/pdf/time-limits-and-gender-differences-on-paper-and-pencil-tests-314jvk7qt5.pdf)

### Inferences
- Gender gap is robust in MR tests but heavily format-dependent; untimed tests and confidence-building framing are cheap mitigations. Training is the practical lever: it raises everyone, and low-baseline groups (often women entering engineering) gain the most in outcomes.
- Avoid framing the subject as innate "talent"; positive/neutral framing appears safer than relying on stereotype-threat claims, which are weakly supported.

### Gaps
- No up-to-date (2020s) cohort trend data on adult MR gaps retrieved.
- Uttal 2013's sex-moderator result (equal gains) is from recall, not verified.

## 6. Measurement: PSVT:R, MRT (Vandenberg–Kuse), Mental Cutting Test

### Takeaway
Standard instruments: MRT (Vandenberg & Kuse 1978; 20 items, choose 2 of 4 correct rotations), PSVT:R (Guay; 30 items, engineering's most-used predictor, used by Sorby as a screener with ~70% cutoff), MCT (25 timed items on cross-sections of solids — closest to technical drawing). All show male advantages; gap size depends on items and timing.

### Cited Findings
- MRT: 20 items, each with a criterion figure and four alternatives, two correct; MCT: timed 25-item test measuring spatial relations/visualization (cut-plane sections); PSVT:R used to predict engineering success for >30 years — [ASEE PEER 18522](https://peer.asee.org/18522.pdf); [CORE](https://core.ac.uk/download/189170072.pdf); [ATE](https://ate.community/downloads/4973/011medina.pdf)
- PSVT:R gender gaps in engineering cohorts: men 79.6 vs women 68.1 (one institution), 79.3 vs 63.2 (another); women less likely to get high MCT scores — [ASEE](https://peer.asee.org/24726.pdf); [EDGJ](https://www.edgj.org/index.php/EDGJ/article/view/115/111)
- MRT gap size depends on item type (Bors & Vigneau 2011; Voyer & Hou 2006) — [Hogrefe](https://econtent.hogrefe.com/doi/10.1027/1614-0001/a000248)
- Geoscience-specific: Geologic Block Cross-sectioning Test used to measure penetrative thinking — [SERC](https://serc.carleton.edu/spatialworkbook/prior_research.html)
- **[recall — verify]** Sorby's program uses PSVT:R score below 60% (some institutions ~70%) as the trigger for the remedial course — [MTU](https://digitalcommons.mtu.edu/michigantech-p/6855/)

### Inferences
- For a drawing course: pre/post with PSVT:R (rotation) + MCT (sections) gives a cheap diagnostic; because control groups improve from retesting alone (Uttal), use parallel forms or a comparison group before claiming tutor effects.

### Gaps
- No psychometric meta-analysis comparing reliability/validity of these tests retrieved; exact Sorby cutoff not confirmed.
