# Why Spatial Thinking Matters: Spatial Ability, Intelligence, STEM Achievement, Creativity and Economic Outcomes

> Research notes, compiled 2026-11-18. Labels used below: **[CORR]** means correlational or predictive (longitudinal but observational); **[CAUSAL]** means experimental or quasi-experimental training evidence; **[ANECDOTE]** means historical or self-report.
> Access caveat: full texts on gwern.net, cdn.vanderbilt.edu, link.springer.com, psychologicalscience.org and edgd.asee.org could not be fetched (the session network blocks those hosts). Many figures below therefore come from abstracts, publisher records or reliable secondary citations, and are flagged where that is the case.

## 1. Longitudinal evidence: Project Talent, SMPY, and spatial ability as "the neglected talent"

### Takeaway
Two large longitudinal datasets, Project Talent (about 400,000 US high-school students tested in 1960) and SMPY (gifted 13-year-olds followed for decades), show that adolescent spatial ability predicts STEM degrees, STEM occupations, patents and STEM publications. It adds prediction beyond math and verbal scores. Because talent searches select almost only on math and verbal tests, they miss most top spatial scorers. All of this evidence is **[CORR]**, though it is prospective and strong.

### Cited Findings
- **[CORR]** Wai, J., Lubinski, D., & Benbow, C. P. (2009). "Spatial ability for STEM domains: Aligning over 50 years of cumulative psychological knowledge solidifies its importance." *Journal of Educational Psychology*, 101(4), 817–835. The paper brings together (a) a pre-1957 NSF report and literature review, (b) Project Talent, a stratified random sample of US Grade 9–12 students tested in 1960 with an 11-year follow-up, and (c) SMPY data on the top 1% in ability, 1971 to the present. It finds that spatial ability distinguishes people who go on to advanced educational and occupational credentials in STEM. — [Vanderbilt PDF (record)](https://cdn.vanderbilt.edu/t2-my/my-prd/wp-content/uploads/sites/826/2013/02/Wai2009SpatialAbility.pdf); [ResearchGate record](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/228627975_Spatial_Ability_for_STEM_Domains_Aligning_Over_50_Years_of_Cumulative_Psychological_Knowledge_Solidifies_Its_Importance)
- **[CORR]** Project Talent covered about 400,000 US high-school students, assessed in 1960 and followed up 11 years later. — [PNAS/PMC description (PMC7382268)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7382268)
- **[CORR]** "45% of all STEM PhDs in their longitudinal study (n = 400,000) were within the top 4% of spatial skills in high school." This is a secondary citation of Wai et al. 2009 by Miller & Halpern and should be checked against the original figure. — [Miller & Halpern, spatial training paper (Northwestern PDF)](https://groups.psych.northwestern.edu/uttal/people/documents/MillerHalpern-inpressfinal-Spatialtraining.pdf)
- **[CORR]** Neglected talent: among students in the top 1% of spatial ability, about 70% were *not* in the top 1% in math or verbal ability. Talent searches that use only math and verbal tests therefore miss most spatially gifted students. — [Wai, KQED MindShift "Why we need to value spatial creativity"](https://kqed.org/mindshift/30294/why-we-need-to-value-spatial-creativity); [Scientific American, "Recognizing Spatial Intelligence"](https://www.scientificamerican.com/article/recognizing-spatial-intel/)
- **[CORR]** Lubinski, D. (2010). "Spatial ability and STEM: A sleeping giant for talent identification and development." *Personality and Individual Differences*, 49, 344–351. It tabulates Project Talent terminal degrees by field, for example 202 physical-science and 71 engineering doctorates. — [Vanderbilt PDF](https://cdn.vanderbilt.edu/t2-my/my-prd/wp-content/uploads/sites/826/2013/02/Lubinski_2010_spatial.pdf); [ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/222408470_Spatial_ability_and_STEM_A_sleeping_giant_for_talent_identification_and_development)
- **[CORR]** Newcombe's summary for teachers: people with high spatial scores in high school were much more likely to major in STEM and to enter STEM careers. — [Newcombe, "Picture This," *American Educator* (AFT)](https://www.aft.org/sites/default/files/Newcombe_1.pdf)
- **[CORR]** Kell, H. J., Lubinski, D., Benbow, C. P., & Steiger, J. H. (2013). "Creativity and technical innovation: Spatial ability's unique role." *Psychological Science*, 24(9), 1831–1836.
  - Sample: 563 SMPY participants, top 0.5% at age 13, given spatial tests in the late 1970s.
  - Result: SAT-Math and SAT-Verbal at 13 predicted publications and patents about 30 years later. Spatial ability gave **incremental validity** beyond them, especially for STEM publications and patents.
  - The authors conclude that spatial ability "plays a key and unique role in structuring many important psychological phenomena."
  — [APS press release](https://www.psychologicalscience.org/?p=87681); [Vanderbilt news](https://news.vanderbilt.edu/2013/12/11/early-spatial-reasoning/); [Psychology Today summary](https://www.psychologytoday.com/us/blog/ulterior-motives/201310/intelligence-spatial-ability-and-creativity)
- Secondary summaries say spatial ability added about 7% of explained variance. This figure comes from a blog, not the paper, so treat it as unverified. — [Psychology Today](https://www.psychologytoday.com/us/blog/ulterior-motives/201310/intelligence-spatial-ability-and-creativity)
- **[CORR]** Lubinski & Benbow (*Nature*, 2016, "How to raise a genius") summarises 45 years of SMPY and argues that spatial ability is a neglected dimension in talent identification. — [Benbow, Nature 2016 (ISIR PDF)](https://www.isironline.org/wp-content/uploads/2016/09/Benbow-NATURE-2016.pdf)
- Popular framing of the "neglect" argument for policy audiences (UK): "By neglecting spatial thinkers, how many Elon Musks have we missed?" — [COBIS blog](https://www.cobis.org.uk/blog/finding-hidden-talents-in-stem); [BESA](https://besa.org.uk/?p=7336)

### Inferences
- Project Talent is nationally representative and SMPY covers the extreme right tail, so the spatial–STEM link holds across the whole ability range, not just among the gifted.
- School selection and assessment (exams, olympiads, gifted programmes) that ignore spatial ability systematically under-identify future engineers and inventors. This is the core policy argument for teaching and assessing spatial skills explicitly, for example through technical drawing (черчение) and solid geometry.

### Gaps
- Exact effect sizes for Kell et al. 2013 (odds ratios, ΔR²) and the original Wai 2009 figure for "45% of STEM PhDs in top 4%" could not be checked because the full-text hosts were blocked.
- Uttal & Cohen's chapter reports a different follow-up length (19 years) for Project Talent than the 11 years in Wai 2009. The 11-year figure comes from the original paper. — [Uttal & Cohen chapter](https://groups.psych.northwestern.edu/uttal/documents/UttalandCohen.pdf)

## 2. Spatial ability in the structure of intelligence and its links to math, physics, chemistry, geology, medicine, surgery, architecture and dentistry

### Takeaway
In the Cattell–Horn–Carroll (CHC) model, spatial ability is the broad factor Gv (visual processing). It sits under g and correlates strongly with fluid reasoning (Gf), yet it carries distinct variance. Across disciplines, spatial measures correlate modestly with performance (r ≈ .25–.35 in math, anatomy, chemistry and laparoscopic surgery; up to about .5 in some geology studies). These are **[CORR]** relationships. The math link survives controls for fluid and verbal ability.

### Cited Findings
**Structure of intelligence**
- In CHC, Gv is "the ability to perceive, analyze, synthesize, and think with visual patterns, including the ability to store and recall visual representations." Its narrow abilities include Visualization, Spatial Relations, Spatial Scanning and Visual Memory. Carroll (1993) placed g at Stratum III above broad abilities such as Gv. — [Wikipedia: CHC theory](https://www.Wikipedia.org/wiki/CHC_theory); [CSUS CHC definitions](https://www.csus.edu/indiv/c/christoc/242A/CHC%20DEFINITIONS.pdf)
- **[CORR]** A reanalysis (Santacreu et al.) found a strong higher-order Gv factor that correlates strongly with fluid ability (Gf). Some researchers doubt that cleanly separable spatial sub-factors exist. — [Fogarty & Burton, factor structure of visual imagery (USQ)](https://research.usq.edu.au/download/fe5a3d14e14959861bf43408038ac28ab4c4195ca5a9a4c93f1241763f4712db/159587/Fogarty_Burton-Fogarty_The_factor_structure_of_visual_imagery.pdf)
- Debate over CHC versus bifactor models of g. — [Canivez & Youngstrom 2019 (EIU PDF)](https://www.ux1.eiu.edu/~glcanivez/Adobe%20pdf/Publications-Papers/Canivez%20&%20Youngstrom%20(2019)%20CHC%20Challenges.pdf)

**Mathematics**
- **[CORR]** Mix & Cheng (2012) review: the spatial–math link holds at several ages and across math measures, including arithmetic. Some researchers argue numbers are represented in spatial formats (the mental number line). — cited in [Emory ETD: Meta-analysis of mental rotation and math](https://etd.library.emory.edu/concern/etds/1831cj95s)
- **[CORR]** Atit, K., Power, J. R., Pigott, T., Lee, J., Geer, E. A., Uttal, D. H., Ganley, C. M., & Sorby, S. A. (2022). "Examining the relations between spatial skills and mathematical performance: A meta-analysis." *Psychonomic Bulletin & Review*, 29(3), 699–720.
  - Scope: 45 articles.
  - Result: a positive, moderate association that is stable across grade and gender. It **persists after controlling for fluid reasoning and verbal skills** (meta-analytic SEM).
  — [Univ. of Limerick record](https://pure.ul.ie/en/publications/examining-the-relations-between-spatial-skills-and-mathematical-p/); [UC Riverside press release](https://news.ucr.edu/articles/2021/11/30/study-confirms-link-between-spatial-and-math-skills)
- **[CORR]** Xie, F., Zhang, L., Chen, X., & Xin, Z. (2020). "Is spatial ability related to mathematical ability: A meta-analysis." *Educational Psychology Review*, 32, 113–155.
  - Scope: 73 studies, 263 effect sizes.
  - A citing paper reports r = .25 [95% CI .21–.29] for visual–spatial skill and arithmetic. This figure is a secondary report.
  — [AMiner record](https://aminer.org/pub/5db9271647c8f766461bd6e0/is-spatial-ability-related-to-mathematical-ability-a-meta-analysis); [Frontiers 2020 citing paper](https://www.frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2020.591308/full)
- **[CORR]** An unpublished (non-peer-reviewed) Emory thesis meta-analysis found mental rotation–math r ≈ .30. — [Emory ETD](https://etd.library.emory.edu/concern/etds/1831cj95s)

**Medicine and surgery**
- **[CORR]** Kramp, K. H., et al. (2016). "The predictive value of aptitude assessment in laparoscopic surgery: a meta-analysis." *Medical Education*, 50(4).
  - Laparoscopic skill correlated with visual-spatial ability at r = 0.32 (95% CI 0.25–0.39).
  - Dynamic 3-D visuospatial tests correlated more strongly than static tests.
  — [Univ. of Groningen record](https://research.rug.nl/en/publications/the-predictive-value-of-aptitude-assessment-in-laparoscopic-surge/)
- **[CORR]** Keehner et al. (2004). Spatial ability correlated with laparoscopic skill in less experienced surgeons (r = .39) but not in experienced ones (r = .02). This suggests spatial ability matters most during *learning*. — [Dundee record](https://discovery.dundee.ac.uk/en/publications/spatial-ability-experience-and-skill-in-laparoscopic-surgery/)
- A 2022 longitudinal study in *Surgical Endoscopy* says the true extent of the influence "remains largely unclear": some studies find the effect fades with practice, others that it lasts. — [Springer, Surg Endosc 2022](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00464-022-09328-1)
- **[CORR]** Roach et al. (2021) meta-analysis in *Anatomical Sciences Education*: spatial ability and anatomy assessment performance, r ≈ 0.24 (15 studies, N = 1,245). — [IUPUI ScholarWorks](https://scholarworks.indianapolis.iu.edu/items/b2a5d1e8-6ca1-4226-bf4d-4e5061fcbd99)
- A 2025 systematic review found that spatial training transfers mainly to *similar* anatomy tasks. — [PMC12686770](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC12686770/)

**Chemistry, geology and dentistry**
- **[CORR]** Correlations reported in a review of older studies (partial excerpts, not checked against the primary studies):
  - Chemistry: r ≈ .2–.35 for spatial visualisation and chemistry (general and organic).
  - Geology: r ≈ .35–.51 for spatial visualisation and introductory geology.
  - Dentistry students: r ≈ .29–.37.
  — [CORE review PDF](https://core.ac.uk/download/236104806.pdf)
- Stieff & Uttal (2015) review whether spatial training actually raises STEM achievement, which correlations alone cannot show. — [Stieff & Uttal 2015 (Northwestern PDF)](https://groups.psych.northwestern.edu/uttal/documents/StieffUttal2015.pdf)

### Inferences
- The typical r ≈ .25–.35 explains roughly 6–12% of variance. That is modest for an individual, but it matters at the population level and for selection at the tails. It is also stronger in novices, which is exactly where school education acts.
- The math link persists after controlling for g, Gf and verbal skills (Atit et al.). This supports treating spatial reasoning as a distinct, teachable target rather than a proxy for general intelligence.

### Gaps
- No primary source was found here for Raven's matrices' spatial loading, Carroll's 1993 factor loadings for specific spatial tests, architecture-specific correlations, or physics-specific meta-analyses.
- The Newcombe / spatial–numerical association (mental number line) literature was not retrieved in detail beyond the Mix & Cheng framing.

## 3. Spatial skills and engineering retention: Sorby's data on women in engineering

### Takeaway
At Michigan Tech, and later at other universities, first-year engineering students who failed a spatial screening test (PSVT:R) were given a short spatial-visualisation course. This raised grades in several STEM courses and improved retention, especially for women. The design is quasi-experimental **[CAUSAL, moderate strength]**: there was no random assignment, and placement depended on a cutoff score.

### Cited Findings
- **[CAUSAL-quasi]** Sorby, S., Veurink, N., & Streiner, S. (2018). "Does spatial skills instruction improve STEM outcomes? The answer is 'yes'." *Learning and Individual Differences*, 67, 209–222.
  - Sample: more than 3,000 first-year engineering students over 5 years.
  - Design: experimental and control groups defined by a pass/fail cutoff on the spatial test.
  - Results: training improved grades across several STEM courses, and the intervention had a positive impact on women's retention in engineering.
  — [Michigan Tech Digital Commons](https://digitalcommons.mtu.edu/michigantech-p/6855/); [Rowan record](https://www.researchwithrowan.com/en/publications/does-spatial-skills-instruction-improve-stem-outcomes-the-answer-/)
- Sorby & Veurink, "Impact of visualization training on student leaving" (67th ASEE EDGD Proceedings) looks at students who *left* engineering. Spatial training had a positive effect on retention within engineering and at the university, particularly for women. — [ASEE EDGD PDF](https://edgd.asee.org/wp-content/uploads/sites/22/2019/09/Impact-of-Visualization-Training-on-Student-Leaving_Sorby-Veurink_67th-EDGD-Proceedings.pdf)
- Related ASEE papers: a community-college extension, and a longitudinal analysis of spatial training in engineering graphics (Blasko et al.). — [ASEE PEER 27719](https://peer.asee.org/27719.pdf); [ASEE PEER 25853](https://peer.asee.org/25853.pdf); [ASEE PEER 18592](https://peer.asee.org/18592.pdf); [Blasko et al., Longitudinal Analysis (ResearchGate)](https://researchgate.net/profile/Dawn-Blasko/publication/266170923_Longitudinal_Analysis_of_Spatial_Skills_Training_in_Engineering_Graphics/links/54cf93ca0cf29ca810fee7e1/Longitudinal-Analysis-of-Spatial-Skills-Training-in-Engineering-Graphics.pdf)

### Inferences
- Engineering graphics and descriptive geometry courses are a natural place for this kind of intervention. Sorby's course grew out of engineering-graphics teaching, which directly supports keeping technical drawing (черчение) in school and first-year curricula.

### Gaps
- Exact retention percentages by gender and condition could not be extracted because the ASEE host was blocked. Commonly quoted figures from earlier Sorby papers (for example, women's retention rising from about 47% to about 77%) were **not verified** in this session and should not be cited without checking.

UNESCO describes a global shortage of engineers, both in numbers and in skills. ## 5. Creativity and invention: visual thinkers, imagery in scientific discovery, aphantasia

### Takeaway
Accounts of visual thinking in discovery (Einstein's light-beam thought experiment, Kekulé's benzene ring) are **[ANECDOTE]** and contested. Shepard (1978) and Miller (1984) argued that imagery mattered, but those claims are not systematic. Kell et al. (2013) give the strongest quantitative evidence linking spatial ability to invention (patents). Imagery vividness is *not* the same as spatial ability: people with aphantasia (no voluntary visual imagery) are over-represented in science, computing and mathematics jobs.

### Cited Findings
- Shepard (1978), Miller (1984, *Imagery in Scientific Thought*) and Tweney et al. (1981) argued that mental imagery played an important role in the thinking of many great scientists. A later review notes these claims "are not based on systematic empirical studies." — [Jim Davies summary of Pearson 2007](https://www.jimdavies.org/summaries/Pearson2007.html); [Brewer & Schommer-Aikins 2006, *Review of General Psychology* 10(2):130](https://https-sage-cnpereading-com-443.webvpn1.xju.edu.cn/doi/10.1037/1089-2680.10.2.130)
- **[ANECDOTE]** Einstein: "the psychical entities which seem to serve as elements in thought are certain signs and more or less clear images." He also described imagining himself riding beside a light beam. — [Pearson 2007 summary](https://www.jimdavies.org/summaries/Pearson2007.html)
- **[ANECDOTE]** Kekulé said the benzene structure came to him as a vision of a snake seizing its own tail. — [same source](https://www.jimdavies.org/summaries/Pearson2007.html)
- Galton (1880) claimed that "men of science" had faint imagery. Brewer & Schommer-Aikins (2006) challenged this. — [Brewer & Schommer-Aikins 2006](https://https-sage-cnpereading-com-443.webvpn1.xju.edu.cn/doi/10.1037/1089-2680.10.2.130)
- **[CORR]** Zeman, A., et al. (2020). "Phantasia—The psychological significance of lifelong visual imagery vividness extremes." *Cortex*, 130, 426–440.
  - Sample: about 2,000 people with aphantasia and about 200 with hyperphantasia.
  - More than 20% of those with aphantasia worked in science, computing or mathematics.
  - More than 25% of those with hyperphantasia worked in arts, design or entertainment.
  - The sample was self-selected (over 14,000 people contacted the team after media coverage).
  — [Zeman et al. 2020 PDF (Edinburgh)](https://medicine-vet-medicine.ed.ac.uk/sites/default/files/2025-10/Research%20article%20-%20Zeman%20et%20al%20-%20Cortex%20-%202020.pdf); [Exeter news](https://news-archive.exeter.ac.uk/2020/may/title_793208_en.html); [BBC Science Focus](https://www.sciencefocus.com/news/people-with-aphantasia-are-more-likely-to-work-in-a-stem-field/)
- Craig Venter says aphantasia helps him assimilate complex information into new ideas. — [STV News](https://news.stv.tv/scotland/people-unable-to-visualise-in-mind-likely-to-work-in-science)
- **[CORR]** Spatial ability at 13 adds prediction of patents and STEM publications beyond SAT-M and SAT-V (Kell et al. 2013; see section 1). — [Vanderbilt news](https://news.vanderbilt.edu/2013/12/11/early-spatial-reasoning/)

### Inferences
- The aphantasia data warn against equating "spatial thinking" with "vivid pictures in the head." Spatial reasoning such as mental rotation, transformation and diagrammatic reasoning can be non-pictorial and schematic. For teaching, this argues for externalising spatial thinking on paper (sketches, projections, diagrams) rather than relying on inner imagery. This fits Shepard's theme of externalising mental images.

### Gaps
- No primary source was retrieved for Tesla's self-reported imagery, Watson & Crick's physical models, or Feynman diagrams as visual reasoning. These are well known historically but not sourced here.
- The exact 1978 Shepard chapter ("Externalization of mental images and the act of creation," in Randhawa & Coffman, eds., *Visual Learning, Thinking, and Communication*) could not be located online in this session.

## 6. Gender gap and whether spatial training narrows STEM gaps

### Takeaway
Male advantage in mental rotation is absent or negligible in preschool. It emerges at about 6–8 years and grows to a moderate size by adolescence **[CORR]**. Spatial skills are malleable: training effects average g ≈ .47, are durable and transfer **[CAUSAL]**. Spatial training also improves math (g ≈ .28) **[CAUSAL]**. Sorby's engineering intervention improved women's retention **[CAUSAL-quasi]**. Direct evidence that training *closes* the gender gap in STEM participation at scale is limited.

### Cited Findings
- **[CORR]** Lauer, J. E., Yhang, E., & Lourenco, S. F. (2019). "The development of gender differences in spatial reasoning: A meta-analytic review." *Psychological Bulletin*, 145(6), 537–565. doi:10.1037/bul0000191.
  - Scope: 303 effect sizes, 30,613 children aged 3–18 (128 studies).
  - Pattern: no difference in preschoolers; a small male advantage emerges at 6–8; it rises to moderate in adolescence.
  - Task characteristics moderate the gap. The causes are not resolved.
  — [Emory news](https://news.emory.edu/stories/2019/04/esc_gender_gap_spatial_reasoning/campus.html); [ScienceDaily](https://www.sciencedaily.com/releases/2019/04/190411154728.htm); [EurekAlert](https://www.eurekalert.org/news-releases/716218)
- **[CAUSAL]** Uttal, D. H., Meadow, N. G., Tipton, E., Hand, L. L., Alden, A. R., Warren, C., & Newcombe, N. S. (2013). "The malleability of spatial skills: A meta-analysis of training studies." *Psychological Bulletin*, 139(2), 352–402.
  - Scope: 217 studies.
  - Pooled training-versus-control effect g = 0.47 (SE 0.04). Gains were durable and transferred.
  - A secondary source reports g = 0.61 for children under 13 (53 studies).
  — [PubMed 22663761](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22663761/); [PMC7379338](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7379338); [Uttal & Newcombe PDF](https://www.gamesandlearning.org/wp-content/uploads/2013/09/metaanalysis-Uttal_Newcombe.pdf)
- **[CAUSAL]** Yang et al. (2020), *Frontiers in Psychology*: spatial training in children aged 0–8 gave g = 0.96 (SE 0.10). — [Frontiers 10.3389/fpsyg.2020.01938](https://www.frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2020.01938/full)
- **[CAUSAL]** Hawes, Z. C. K., Gilligan-Lee, K. A., & Mix, K. S. (2022). "Effects of spatial training on mathematics performance: A meta-analysis." *Developmental Psychology*, 58(1), 112–137.
  - Scope: 29 controlled pre-post studies, N = 3,765, 89 effect sizes.
  - Effect on math: g = .28 (SE .07). Effect on spatial skill itself: g = .49.
  - Effects increase with age (3–20). Concrete manipulatives work better than computerised training.
  - No significant publication bias.
  - The authors interpret this as evidence for a causal role of spatial thinking in math, while noting that transfer mechanisms are poorly understood.
  — [Surrey Open Research](https://openresearch.surrey.ac.uk/esploro/outputs/journalArticle/Effects-of-Spatial-Training-on-Mathematics/99783428802346); [Surrey press release](https://www.surrey.ac.uk/news/spatial-training-blocks-and-puzzles-could-unlock-uks-mathematical-potential); [EurekAlert](https://www.eurekalert.org/news-releases/943021)
- **[CAUSAL-quasi]** Sorby et al. (2018): spatial training improved retention of women in engineering (see section 3). — [Michigan Tech](https://digitalcommons.mtu.edu/michigantech-p/6855/)

### Inferences
- The gender gap appears during the school years, the same period when it can be trained (g ≈ .5–1.0). This argues for systematic spatial curricula in primary and lower-secondary school (constructive geometry, drawing, projections, models) rather than remediation only at university.
- Hawes et al. found that concrete manipulatives beat computerised training. This favours hands-on drawing and model-building, the traditional черчение approach, over pure app-based training.

### Gaps
- No randomised large-scale study was found showing that spatial training closes the gender gap in *STEM degree completion or careers*. The evidence chain (training improves spatial skill, which predicts STEM outcomes) is indirect except for Sorby's quasi-experimental retention data.
- Uttal 2013 is often reported as finding similar training gains for males and females. That point was not checked here.
