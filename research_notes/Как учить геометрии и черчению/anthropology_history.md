# Anthropology, Evolution and History of Spatial Cognition, Geometry and Technical Drawing

Scope note: compiled from ~20 web searches (Nov 2026). Most sources are abstracts, publisher/institutional pages, press releases and reputable science journalism; full texts of several key papers were not read. Items marked "(unverified)" or listed under Gaps should not be stated as fact without checking.

## 1. Deep prehistory: marks, symmetry, toolmaking brains and teaching

### Takeaway
The archaeological record shows deliberate geometric marking long before figurative art (Trinil shell ~430–540 ka, attributed to *Homo erectus*; Blombos engraved ochre ~100–75 ka; Blombos ochre-crayon cross-hatching ~73 ka), and neuroarchaeology experiments show that learning to make stone tools reshapes fronto-parietal brain networks and is transmitted far better by teaching and language than by imitation. Interpretations ("symbol", "art", "intended symmetry") are contested and must be flagged.

### Cited Findings
**Oldest geometric marks**
- Trinil, Java: one of 166 freshwater mussel shells excavated in the 1890s (Dubois collection) bears a deliberate zigzag engraving (M-shape, parallel lines, reversed N); animal or weathering origin excluded by the team; dated between ~540,000 and ~430,000 years ago; associated with *Homo erectus*. Published Nature online Dec 2014 (print 2015, Joordens et al.). — [Science News](https://www.sciencenews.org/article/human-ancestors-engraved-abstract-patterns); [NBC News](https://www.nbcnews.com/science/science-news/old-master-540-000-year-old-shell-has-oldest-ever-n261171)
- John Shea: "If Homo erectus carved geometric designs into shells half a million years ago, then geometric engravings cannot be considered 'modern human behavior'" — but cautions that only a second similarly marked shell from a new excavation would confirm it. — [Science News](https://www.sciencenews.org/article/human-ancestors-engraved-abstract-patterns)
- Blombos Cave engraved ochre: Henshilwood, d'Errico & Watts (2009, *Journal of Human Evolution* 57(1)) report 13 additional incised ochre pieces from levels ~100,000–75,000 years old; designs include cross-hatching, fan shapes and wavy lines; eight of the 13 came from levels dated to ~100 ka. — [USF Digital Commons record](https://digitalcommons.usf.edu/kip_articles/1784); [Science News](https://www.sciencenews.org/article/engraved-pigments-point-ancient-symbolic-tradition); [Balter blog summarising Science report](https://michael-balter.blogspot.com/2009/02/earlier-start-for-human-art.html)
- Science News framed it as a geometric-design *tradition* lasting ~25,000 years (100–75 ka), while noting claims of symbolism at ~100 ka are controversial. — [Science News](https://www.sciencenews.org/article/engraved-pigments-point-ancient-symbolic-tradition)
- Blombos also yielded a ~100,000-year-old ochre-processing "workshop" with a liquefied ochre mixture stored in two abalone shells (2011). — [USF Digital Commons](https://digitalcommons.usf.edu/kip_articles/114)
- "Oldest drawing": Henshilwood et al. 2018, *Nature* 562:115–118 (doi:10.1038/s41586-018-0514-3): cross-hatched pattern drawn with an ochre crayon on a ground silcrete flake from ~73,000-year-old MSA levels; experiments compared paint vs crayon and favored crayon. Same layer held ochre-coated shell beads and engraved ochre. — [IDEAS/RePEc record of Nature paper](https://ideas.repec.org/a/nat/nature/v562y2018i7725d10.1038_s41586-018-0514-3.html); [Sci.News](https://www.sci.news/archaeology/earliest-drawing-human-blombos-cave-06408.html); [UNIGE open archive](https://archive-ouverte.unige.ch/unige:122885)
- Lines appear abruptly truncated at the flake edges, suggesting the pattern originally extended over a larger surface (reported in coverage; check paper). — [NPR](https://www.npr.org/2018/09/12/647178335/researchers-discover-ancient-ha) (unverified detail)
- Henshilwood himself: "We don't know that it's art at all... We know that it's a symbol." Margaret Conkey criticized words like "drawing" and "crayon" as rhetoric implying modern behavior. — [NPR](https://www.npr.org/2018/09/12/647178335/researchers-discover-ancient-ha)

**Handaxe symmetry and spatial cognition**
- Wynn (1995, 2002) treats the imposition of form, especially symmetry, on Acheulean handaxes as a cognitive leap in shape recognition and spatial thinking. — [Thomas G. Wynn, Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_G._Wynn)
- Wynn & Coolidge's "Enhanced Working Memory" hypothesis: a small heritable increase in working memory/executive function may underlie the modern mind (and Homo sapiens' success vs other hominins). — [Thomas G. Wynn, Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_G._Wynn)
- Contested: some argue symmetry is a by-product of working opposed edges in a rule-based way; others (e.g., in *Antiquity* 2018, "Symmetry is its own reward") argue symmetry was deliberately sought and valued in the Middle Pleistocene. — [Antiquity article PDF, Cambridge Core](https://core-prod.cambridgecore.org/core/services/aop-cambridge-core/content/view/B9CE356FBF387AE0CC1C724FEFD26DF0/S0003598X18000352a.pdf/symmetry-is-its-own-reward-on-the-character-and-significance-of-acheulean-handaxe-symmetry-in-the-middle-pleistocene.pdf)

**Neuroarchaeology: toolmaking changes the brain (Stout lab)**
- Hecht, Stout et al. 2015 (*Brain Structure and Function*): after a ~2-year Paleolithic toolmaking training program, white-matter fractional anisotropy changed in branches of the superior longitudinal fasciculus feeding left supramarginal gyrus, bilateral ventral precentral gyri and right inferior frontal gyrus (pars triangularis); FA rose during intense training and fell when training was reduced — i.e., the brain changed with practice, like any skill. — [PMC4398452](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4398452)
- Stout et al. 2015 (*PLOS ONE*): fMRI linked prefrontal activity during technical judgments to handaxe-making success — "complex cognition shaped the Stone Age hand axe." — [Neuroscience News](https://neurosciencenews.com/higher-executive-function-evolution-tools-1958/)
- Hecht, Pargeter, Khreisheh & Stout 2023 (*Scientific Reports*): prior craft experience improved initial toolmaking performance and boosted training-related neuroplasticity in a fronto-parietal white-matter pathway for action control. — [Scientific Reports](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1038/s41598-023-29994-y); [PMC9938911](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9938911)
- Note: the requested "Stout & Hecht 2018" paper on handaxe training could not be located; the above are the verified closest matches.

**Teaching and the evolution of pedagogy**
- Morgan et al. 2015, *Nature Communications* 6:6029 (doi:10.1038/ncomms7029): 184 adult volunteers in transmission chains learned Oldowan flake production under five conditions (reverse engineering, imitation/emulation, basic teaching, gestural teaching, verbal teaching). Across six measures, transmission improved with teaching and especially with language, not with imitation or emulation. — [PubMed 25585382](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25585382/); [St Andrews "School of Rock"](https://news.st-andrews.ac.uk/archive/school-of-rock/)
- Authors argue low-fidelity social learning may explain Oldowan stasis (~700,000 years) and that teaching or proto-language may have been a prerequisite for the Acheulean (~1.7 Ma). Morgan: Oldowan hominins were "probably not talking." — [UPI](https://www.upi.com/Science_News/2015/01/15/First-human-conversations-were-probably-about-rocks/5831421347377/); [Liverpool news](https://news.liverpool.ac.uk/?p=49704)

### Inferences
- Narrative hook for descriptive geometry: geometric marking (lines, cross-hatching, parallels) precedes pictures of animals by tens of thousands of years — "humans drew lines before they drew things."
- Stout's and Morgan's results together give a strong educational message: spatial-technical skill is trainable (brain plasticity) and transmitted best by explicit teaching with language — the very situation of a drawing class.

### Gaps
- Full Morgan et al. effect sizes not extracted.
- The exact Wynn & Coolidge Cambridge Archaeological Journal paper was not located; cite their books (e.g., *How to Think Like a Neandertal*, 2012; *The Rise of Homo sapiens*, 2009) after checking.

## 2. History of plans, projections and technical drawing

### Takeaway
Scaled plans are at least ~4,100 years old (Gudea of Lagash, statue "Architect with Plan," c. 2120–2100 BC; Egyptian tomb plan of Ramesses IV, c. 1150 BC); Vitruvius (1st c. BC) named plan/elevation/perspective; Brunelleschi's lost panel (c. 1413–1425) made perspective a demonstrable geometric procedure; Monge (1760s–1790s) turned orthogonal projection into a rigorous science, first kept as a military secret; it entered Russia via the Institute of the Corps of Railway Engineers, where Sevastyanov published the first Russian textbook (1821).

### Cited Findings
**Mesopotamia and Egypt**
- Gudea Statue B (Louvre AO 2), seated diorite, headless, nicknamed "Architect with Plan": the tablet on his lap carries the plan of the Eninnu temple of Ningirsu at Girsu, with a measuring scale and stylus; Lagash II, dated c. 2130–2110 BC (Münster) / c. 2120 BC / c. 2100 BC; it bears the longest inscription on any Gudea statue. Statue F shows an empty tablet. — [Universität Münster](https://www.uni-muenster.de/Altoriental/institut/sammlung/foyer/Gudea_B.html); [CDLI wiki "architecte au plan"](https://cdli.ox.ac.uk/wiki/architecte_au_plan); [Smarthistory](https://smarthistory.org/?p=22863)
- The Gudea cylinders describe the god/hero showing a lapis-lazuli tablet with a house plan drawn on it — the plan as divinely revealed. — [Gudea cylinders, Wikipedia](https://www.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gudea_cylinders)
- Caution: no source found calling Gudea's plan "the oldest architectural plan"; older Mesopotamian plan tablets may exist (see CDLI). — [CDLI wiki](https://cdli.ox.ac.uk/wiki/architecte_au_plan)
- Turin papyrus plan of the tomb of Ramesses IV (KV2), 20th Dynasty (c. 1150 BC): ground plan with hieratic labels and dimensions, papyrus ~24.5 × 86 cm; roughly 1:28 scale (per Wikipedia, unverified against Carter & Gardiner 1917, *JEA*); shows the sarcophagus within four nested shrines. A rough limestone ostracon plan was also found near the tomb. — [KV2, Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.com/wiki/KV2); [Egyptian journal article on ancient plans](https://mfth.journals.ekb.eg/article_281764_1b58f5436a39837f778076a75cdbb055.pdf?lang=en); [Griffith Institute archive](https://archive.griffith.ox.ac.uk/index.php/carter-collection-i-g-55)
- Do not confuse with the Turin Papyrus *Map* (c. 1150 BC, scribe Amennakhte), a geological/topographic map of the Wadi Hammamat quarry route — often called the oldest surviving geological map. — [History Today](https://www.historytoday.com/archive/cartography/turin-papyrus-map-c1150-bc)

**Vitruvius**
- *De architectura* I.2.2: design (dispositio) has three species, in Greek *ideai*: *ichnographia* (plan, drawn with compass and rule), *orthographia* (elevation of the front), *scaenographia* (shaded perspective-like view with sides receding, lines converging to a centre). — [Lexundria, Vitr. 1.2.2 parallel translations](https://lexundria.com/vitr/1.2.2/cf); [Morgan translation, Project Gutenberg](https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/20239/pg20239.html)

**Brunelleschi**
- Antonio Manetti's (late 15th c.) biography describes Brunelleschi's painted panel of the Florence Baptistery with a hole at the vanishing point; the viewer looked through the back of the panel at a mirror to compare painting and real building. Panel lost. Dates proposed: Gioseffi 1401–09, Kemp before 1413, Edgerton 1425; often given as c. 1415–1420. — [WebExhibits "Arrow in the Eye"](https://www.webexhibits.org/arrowintheeye/brunelleschi1.html); [Perspective (graphical), Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.com/wiki/%E2%8C%86)
- Contested: Manetti never calls it an "experiment"; some details conflict (field of view); Malcolm Park argues it worked as a projection device. Alberti's *De pictura* (1435) codified the construction. — [WebExhibits](https://www.webexhibits.org/arrowintheeye/brunelleschi1.html); [Park article (Scribd copy)](https://www.scribd.com/document/597046347/23468273)

**Monge and the military secret**
- As a young draftsman at the École royale du génie de Mézières, Monge solved a fortification *défilement* problem (arranging works so defenders are shielded from fire from outside heights) graphically, faster than the arithmetic methods; this became descriptive geometry. He was ordered to keep the method secret; duration given variously as "several years" up to ~25 years (secondary sources disagree). — [Gaspard Monge, Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gaspard_Monge); [EBSCO Research Starters](https://ebsco.com/research-starters/history/gaspard-monge); [Encyclopedia.com (DSB)](https://encyclopedia.com/science/encyclopedias-almanacs-transcripts-and-maps/gaspard-monge-0)
- After secrecy lifted, it was taught at the École normale (1795) and École polytechnique and spread to West Point (founded 1802). Smithsonian holds the Jullien models used to teach it. — [Gaspard Monge, Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gaspard_Monge); [Smithsonian, Jullien models](https://www.si.edu/spotlight/geometric-models-jullien-models-for-descriptive-geometry/introduction)

**Russia**
- Descriptive geometry was taught at the Institute of the Corps of Railway Engineers (Институт корпуса инженеров путей сообщения, St Petersburg, founded 1809/1810, with French staff); PGUPS dates its graphics teaching tradition 210 years back. — [naukaru / ПГУПС article](https://naukaru.ru/en/nauka/article/46783/jats)
- Prof. Ya. A. Sevastyanov's textbook (1821) was the first descriptive geometry textbook published in Russian (secondary methodological source). — [Иркутский ГАУ, методическое пособие (PDF)](https://irsau.ru/sveden/files/35.03.10/mu/35.03.10_%d0%911.%d0%9e.05.06_%d0%9c%d0%9c_%d0%9d%d0%b0%d1%87%d0%b5%d1%80%d1%82.%d0%b3%d0%b5%d0%be%d0%bc%d0%b5%d1%82%d1%80..pdf)
- Chasles' historical overview (Russian translation on Wikisource) discusses Monge's school's spread. — [Шаль, «Исторический обзор...», Wikisource](https://ru.wikisource.org/wiki/%D0%A1%D1%82%D1%80%D0%B0%D0%BD%D0%B8%D1%86%D0%B0:%D0%98%D1%81%D1%82%D0%BE%D1%80%D0%B8%D1%87%D0%B5%D1%81%D0%BA%D0%B8%D0%B9_%D0%BE%D0%B1%D0%B7%D0%BE%D1%80_%D0%BF%D1%80%D0%BE%D0%B8%D1%81%D1%85%D0%BE%D0%B6%D0%B4%D0%B5%D0%BD%D0%B8%D1%8F_%D0%B8_%D1%80%D0%B0%D0%B7%D0%B2%D0%B8%D1%82%D0%B8%D1%8F_%D0%B3%D0%B5%D0%BE%D0%BC%D0%B5%D1%82%D1%80%D0%B8%D1%87%D0%B5%D1%81%D0%BA%D0%B8%D1%85_%D0%BC%D0%B5%D1%82%D0%BE%D0%B4%D0%BE%D0%B2_(%D0%A8%D0%B0%D0%BB%D1%8C)_1.djvu/221)

### Inferences
- Story arc: plan as sacred revelation (Gudea) -> plan as working document (Ramesses IV) -> codified views (Vitruvius) -> perspective as geometry (Brunelleschi/Alberti) -> projection as science and state secret (Monge) -> engineering language taught nationally (Polytechnique, Russian railway corps).
- The Monge secrecy story is the strongest hook: a drawing method was considered a weapon.

### Gaps
- Sevastyanov's first name/patronymic: sources variously given (Яков Александрович commonly); verify, plus exact title («Основания начертательной геометрии», 1821) and role of French teachers (Potier, Bazaine). Not confirmed by search.
- No sources found on the Bauman (MVTU) school of descriptive geometry; Dürer (*Underweysung der Messung*, 1525) and Booker's *A History of Engineering Drawing* (1963) were not checked; "drawing as the language of the engineer"/blueprint (cyanotype, 1842 Herschel) not verified.
- "Nebra" sky disc is not a plan; not researched.

## 3. Cross-cultural spatial cognition

### Takeaway
Untutored Mundurukú adults and children use core geometric concepts (points, lines, parallelism, right angles) and make Euclidean predictions; languages like Guugu Yimithirr force constant absolute (cardinal) orientation; Micronesian etak navigation imagines the canoe stationary and islands moving; Inuit wayfinding is being transformed by GPS.

### Cited Findings
- Dehaene, Izard, Pica & Spelke 2006, *Science* 311:381–384: two nonverbal tests (incl. "odd-one-out" among six figures) with Mundurukú of the Cururu river (fieldwork 2004–05); children and adults spontaneously used points, lines, parallelism, right angles, and could use geometric maps; authors conclude core geometry is "a universal constituent of the human mind." — [ScienceDaily](https://www.sciencedaily.com/releases/2006/01/060123074357.htm); [EurekAlert](https://www.eurekalert.org/news-releases/622473); [PhilArchive PDF](https://philarchive.org/archive/DEHCKO-2)
- Izard, Pica, Spelke & Dehaene 2011, *PNAS* 108(24):9782–9787: Mundurukú (language lacks words for right angle, parallel) judged that parallel lines never meet and that triangle angles sum to a constant (triangle-completion task, shown with hands), in planar and spherical tasks; conclusion: Euclidean intuitions develop spontaneously in childhood without schooling. — [PMC3116380](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3116380); [ScienceDaily](https://www.sciencedaily.com/releases/2011/05/110525115902.htm); [Discover](https://www.discovermagazine.com/planet-earth/jungle-geometry-who-needs-euclid)
- Izard later: results compatible with either innate geometry emerging ~age 7, or geometry learned from everyday experience. — [Science News](https://sciencenews.org/?p=7515)
- Sablé-Meyer et al. 2021, *PNAS* 118(16): in an "intruder" task with 11 quadrilaterals, all human groups (French adults, kindergartners, uneducated Himba adults of Namibia) were faster/more accurate for regular shapes (square, rectangle); baboons showed no regularity effect even after extensive training; CNNs mimicked baboons, a symbolic Euclidean model fitted humans. Contested: de Waal suggested humans simply live amid right angles. — [PMC8072260](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8072260); [Collège de France](https://college-de-france.fr/en/news/humans-have-unique-sense-of-geometry)
- Guugu Yimithirr (Hopevale, Queensland): almost completely absolute spatial language; speakers must always know cardinal bearings and dead-reckon unseen locations; one would say "there's an ant on your south leg." — [Levinson 1997, J. Linguistic Anthropology (MPI PDF)](https://pure.mpg.de/pubman/item/item_66622_3/component/file_66623/1997_Spatial_description_in_Guugu_Yimithirr.pdf); [MPI record](https://forms.mpi.nl/node/45266)
- Contested: Haviland (1998, *Ethos*) questions how cleanly GY fits the pure absolute type. — [Haviland, UCSD PDF](https://pages.ucsd.edu/%7Ejhaviland/Publications/ETHOSw.Diags.pdf)
- Etak (Caroline Islands, Puluwat): navigator imagines canoe fixed and a reference island ~50 miles off course moving along a sequence of star bearings; each step is one etak (Gladwin 1970). Hutchins (1983; Hutchins & Hinton 1984 "Why the islands move"; *Cognition in the Wild* 1995) argues the "moving islands" frame reduces computational complexity. No full consensus on how etak works. — [Hutchins, UCSD](https://pages.ucsd.edu/~ehutchins/MicroNav.html); [Riesenberg 1975, Micronesica](https://micronesica.org/sites/default/files/the_ghost_islands_of_the_carolines_by_riesenberg_s.h._-_micronesica_vol.11_no.1_jul._1975_o.pdf); [ScienceAlert](https://www.sciencealert.com/were-still-slowly-learning-how-polynesian-voyagers-navigated-entire-oceans)
- Inuit (Igloolik): Aporta & Higgs 2005, "Satellite Culture," *Current Anthropology* 46(5):729–753 — GPS as a "Borgmannian device" that removes engagement with the environment; concerns about loss of traditional wayfinding skill. — [CAS catalog record](https://katalog.lib.cas.cz/KNAV/EdsRecord/edsjsr,edsjsr.10.1086.432651); [McGill Indigenous GIS summary](https://rose.geog.mcgill.ca/wordpress/index84c1.html?p=4311)

### Inferences
- Geometric "primitives" look universal, but which frame of reference is habitual is culturally/linguistically shaped — a useful nuance: descriptive geometry trains a specific, culturally developed viewer-centred projection system on top of universal intuitions.
- Etak is a striking parallel to choosing a reference frame/plane in descriptive geometry (changing planes of projection = changing what is held fixed).

### Gaps
- Pirahã number/space data not researched (Gordon 2004 Science; Frank et al. 2008) — verify before use.
- Specific accuracy numbers for Mundurukú tasks not extracted.
- Evidence on GPS-induced decline of Inuit skill is ethnographic/qualitative; no quantitative study found.

## 4. Is geometry innate?

### Takeaway
There is good evidence for evolutionarily old, non-symbolic geometric systems (reorientation by room shape in rats and toddlers; shape/form sensitivity), but the strong "encapsulated geometric module" claim has been weakened, and abstract Euclidean geometry appears to require the combination of core systems with language/symbols and schooling — Dehaene's group even argues symbolic geometric "language of thought" may be uniquely human.

### Cited Findings
- Cheng (1986): disoriented rats searched using the shape of a rectangular box, ignoring salient features; proposed a geometric module. Hermer & Spelke (1994, 1996): 18–24-month-olds similarly reoriented by room geometry, ignoring a blue wall; adults used both. — [Twyman & Newcombe review, CCBR vol. 8](https://comparative-cognition-and-behavior-reviews.org/vol8_twyman_nardi_newcombe/); [Nardini et al. 2008 Cognition PDF](https://markonardini.webspace.durham.ac.uk/wp-content/uploads/sites/190/2021/04/nardini_cognition2008.pdf)
- Hermer-Vazquez, Spelke & Katsnelson 1999: adults performing verbal shadowing fell back on geometry-only reorientation, suggesting language helps combine geometry and features. — [Hermer-Vazquez et al. 1999 PDF, Harvard LDS](https://www.harvardlds.org/wp-content/uploads/2017/01/hermer1999-1.pdf)
- Challenges: children in larger rooms combine geometry and color; with redesigned tasks 18–24-month-olds use wall color; Newcombe's "adaptive combination" alternative; module "in doubt." — [Twyman & Newcombe](https://comparative-cognition-and-behavior-reviews.org/vol8_twyman_nardi_newcombe/); [PMC4122240](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4122240)
- Lee & Spelke 2010 and Dillon & Spelke: two core systems — navigation by distance/direction of extended surfaces, and object/form recognition by relative length and angle — which Euclidean geometry later unites. — [Lee & Spelke 2010 PDF](https://www.harvardlds.org/wp-content/uploads/2017/01/Lee-and-Spelke-2010-1.pdf); [Dillon & Spelke 2018 PDF](https://www.harvardlds.org/wp-content/uploads/2022/06/DillonSpelke_2018.pdf)
- Baboon vs human shape regularity (Sablé-Meyer 2021) suggests a uniquely human symbolic coding of geometric regularity. — [PMC8072260](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8072260)

### Inferences
- Best synthesis for teaching: we inherit intuitions (shape of space, angles, lengths, parallels) but formal projection and proof are cultural tools that must be taught — consistent with Morgan's teaching results and Stout's plasticity data.

### Gaps
- Infant habituation studies on angle/shape (e.g., Newcombe, Spelke "infants' sensitivity to geometry") not directly checked.

## 5. Surprising connections

### Takeaway
Several story threads connect geometry, drawing and the human mind, but they were only partially verified in this session.

### Cited Findings
- The Trinil zigzag implies geometric marking is not unique to *Homo sapiens*. — [Scientific American blog](https://blogs.scientificamerican.com/observations/world-s-oldest-engraving-upends-theory-of-homo-sapiens-uniqueness/?print=true)
- Gudea's plan tablet and the cylinder myth of a god revealing a plan link plan-drawing with royal/divine authority in the same era as early writing. — [Smarthistory](https://smarthistory.org/?p=22863); [Gudea cylinders](https://www.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gudea_cylinders)
- Hutchins' study of navigation founded "distributed cognition" — cognition embedded in tools and representations (charts, drawings). — [Hutchins, UCSD](https://pages.ucsd.edu/~ehutchins/MicroNav.html)

### Inferences
- Drawing can be framed as "external working memory" (link Wynn & Coolidge to Hutchins): plans let humans hold more spatial information than the mind alone.

### Gaps
- Plato's *Meno* (82b–85b, Socrates leading an enslaved boy to double the square via the diagonal) as "first recorded pedagogy experiment" — well known but not source-checked here; cite Perseus text after verification. Note the episode is Socrates' argument for recollection (anamnesis), i.e., an ancient "innateness" claim — a neat bridge to Spelke.
- Euclid's *Elements* as model of rigor (Spinoza, Newton's *Principia*, Lincoln's study of Euclid) — not verified.
- Geometric signs and the origin of writing (e.g., von Petzinger's catalogue of 32 recurring Paleolithic signs; Schmandt-Besserat's tokens) — not researched.
